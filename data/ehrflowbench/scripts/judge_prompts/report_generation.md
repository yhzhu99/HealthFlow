## Background

Your task is to rigorously assess the quality of a report generated by an AI Coding Agent that was given a EHR data analysis task.

You will be provided with:
- A reference report PDF.
- The agent's generated report PDF.

## Evaluation Rubric

You must evaluate the agent's submission on the following four scoring dimensions. For each dimension, provide a score (1–5).

### 1. method_soundness (Methodology & Analytical Rigor)

Evaluate the scientific soundness and appropriateness of the entire analytical pipeline: data preprocessing, feature engineering, model selection, validation strategy, and result interpretation. This dimension heavily weights the quality and completeness of the agent's justification for its methodological choices (the "Why"), not merely what was done (the "What").

- 5 (Exemplary): Meets all criteria for a score of 4. Additionally, the methodology justification is exceptionally insightful—e.g., comparing the chosen approach against viable alternatives with reasoned trade-offs, providing nuanced interpretation of results within the medical/clinical context, and offering a thoughtful discussion of limitations and their potential impact on conclusions.
- 4 (Strong): The analytical pipeline is sound, appropriate for the data and task, and demonstrates clear evidence of successful execution (e.g., printed metrics, generated output files). The report includes a genuine methodology justification: a clear explanation of why the chosen methods are suitable for this specific problem. Preprocessing, modeling, and evaluation steps are logically coherent and well-integrated.
- 3 (Acceptable): This is the absolute maximum for any submission that lacks execution evidence. The submission presents a well-reasoned analytical plan with appropriate method choices and a clear strategic rationale, but results are not demonstrated or are only partially shown. Alternatively, execution succeeded but the justification is superficial.
- 2 (Weak): The submission provides code or a procedural description but fails to justify methodological choices. This also applies if the method contains significant flaws (e.g., data leakage, inappropriate model for the data type, no validation strategy), or execution was attempted but failed with unresolved errors.
- 1 (Poor): The method is fundamentally flawed or irrelevant to the task. There is no meaningful methodology or justification of any kind.

### 2. presentation_quality (Report Clarity, Structure & Completeness)

Evaluate the overall quality of the final report as a self-contained document. Assess its clarity, logical structure, narrative flow, completeness, and professional formatting.

- 5 (Exemplary): The report is exceptionally clear, professional, and reads as a polished, self-contained analytical document. It features a logical narrative arc (Introduction → Data Description → Methodology → Results → Discussion/Conclusion), effective use of formatting, precise language, and a compelling synthesis of findings with appropriate caveats for the medical context.
- 4 (Strong): The report is well-structured and complete, with a clear logical flow. All major sections are present and well-developed. Language is clear and precise, with only minor room for refinement.
- 3 (Acceptable): The core message and findings are communicated clearly. The report has an adequate structure, though it may suffer from minor organizational inconsistencies or areas where more explanation would be beneficial.
- 2 (Weak): The report is disorganized, incomplete, or difficult to follow. Key sections may be missing or poorly developed. The output resembles a code log rather than a coherent report.
- 1 (Poor): The submission fails to produce a coherent report. It is raw code output, an unstructured collection of fragments, or effectively unreadable.

### 3. artifact_generation (Code Quality & Deliverable Completeness)

Evaluate the functionality, correctness, organization, and completeness of all generated artifacts (code scripts, output data files, saved plots, etc.).

- 5 (Exemplary): Meets all criteria for a score of 4. Additionally, the code demonstrates exemplary software engineering practices: modular design, comprehensive commenting/docstrings, robust error handling, and high reusability. The overall project structure is clean and intuitive.
- 4 (Strong): All required artifacts are generated, correct, and functional. Code is well-organized and logically structured. Outputs are appropriately saved into distinct, well-named files. The code runs without errors and produces the expected outputs.
- 3 (Acceptable): Core artifacts are generated and largely functional. The code implements the required logic but may need minor corrections to run. Output files are generated but may not be perfectly organized.
- 2 (Weak): Artifacts are generated but contain significant errors, are incomplete, or are poorly organized. The code may have logical flaws that produce incorrect results, or key output files are missing.
- 1 (Poor): Fails to generate the required artifacts, or the generated artifacts are fundamentally broken, empty, or irrelevant to the task.

### 4. overall_score (Holistic Assessment)

Provide your holistic assessment of the entire submission. This is not a simple average of the above scores. Use your expert judgment to weigh the dimensions based on the specific task context. A critical failure in any key dimension should heavily penalize this score.

- 5 (Exemplary): A model submission. Achieves a score of 5 on method_soundness and at least 4 on all other dimensions.
- 4 (Strong): A high-quality submission. Achieves 4 or higher across all dimensions; or achieves 5 in one or two key areas while maintaining at least 3 in others, with no critical weaknesses.
- 3 (Acceptable): A competent, complete submission that meets baseline expectations. Achieves at least 3 across all dimensions with no score below 2.
- 2 (Weak): A below-expectations submission. Scores 2 or below in one or more key dimensions, but is not a complete failure.
- 1 (Poor): A fundamentally flawed submission. Scores 1 in any key dimension, or fails to address the task in a meaningful way.
